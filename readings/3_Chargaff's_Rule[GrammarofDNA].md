# Chargaff's Rule: *A Grammar of DNA*

> “Established by **Erwin Chargaff** in the 1950s, these rules describe the base-pairing relationships in double-stranded DNA and were crucial for discovering the **double helix structure**.”

---

## The Four Nitrogenous Bases

DNA is made up of four bases: **Adenine (A), Thymine (T), Guanine (G),** and **Cytosine (C)**.

- **A** and **G** → **Purines** (large, *double-ring* structures)  
- **T** and **C** → **Pyrimidines** (small, *single-ring* structures)

---

## The Pairing Rules

- **A** always pairs with **T** → *2 Hydrogen Bonds*  
- **G** always pairs with **C** → *3 Hydrogen Bonds*

---

## Chargaff’s Ratios

- **Amount of Purines = Amount of Pyrimidines**  
  `A + G = T + C`

- **Amount of Adenine = Amount of Thymine**  
  `A = T`

- **Amount of Guanine = Amount of Cytosine**  
  `G = C`

---

## Example Calculation

Let’s say **A = 30%**

As per Chargaff’s rules:

| Explanation | Result |
|--------------|--------|
| A = T | T = 30% |
| A + T = 60% | Remaining = 40% |
| G = C | G = 20%, C = 20% |
| Check: A + G = T + C | 30% + 20% = 30% + 20% |

## Insight
# Base Pairing Principle

**Purines always pair with Pyrimidines.**  
**Purine–Purine** pairs never occur because they would make the DNA **too wide**, the two large, double-ring structures would **bulge** the helix.  
Similarly, **Pyrimidine–Pyrimidine** pairs never occur because they would make the DNA **too narrow.**  

This complementary pairing maintains a **uniform diameter** throughout the DNA double helix, ensuring its **structural stability**.


Chargaff’s discovery revealed that **DNA maintains a precise balance between purines and pyrimidines**, forming the foundation for **Watson and Crick’s double helix model**.  
This balance ensures DNA’s **structural stability** and **replication accuracy**. 

Chargaff’s balance between bases tells us how mutations disrupt that balance.
When mutations break pairing rules (such as A pairing incorrectly with C), genetic instability occurs, leading to diseases ranging from cancer to viral mutations.
